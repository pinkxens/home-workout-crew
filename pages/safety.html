Standing Workout Safety Guide

Use these as your standing-workout safety rules. The main principle is: form first, then range of motion, speed, and weight, in that order.

Equipment and setup

  • Wear supportive athletic shoes with a grippy sole. Don't exercise in socks on porcelain tile.
  • Clear enough space to step forward, backward, and sideways without hitting furniture.
  • Keep a sturdy counter or chair nearby for balance. Place the chair against a wall; don't use one with wheels.
  • Begin strength workouts with body weight, water bottles, or 2 to 5 lb dumbbells. The final repetitions should feel challenging, but your form should remain controlled.
  • Position your screen directly in front of you so you aren't repeatedly turning your neck.
  • Have water and a towel nearby.
  • Home-based resistance exercise using body weight, bands, or weights can be effective; consistency matters more than elaborate equipment.

Source: ACSM resistance-training guidance

Form checkpoints

  • Stand tall with your ribs over your hips, not leaning backward.
  • Keep shoulders relaxed and gently tighten your abdominal muscles.
  • Maintain "soft" knees rather than locking them.
  • Keep your heel, big-toe base, and little-toe base grounded.
  • During squats and lunges, let your knees travel in the same direction as your toes.
  • Use a hip hinge when bending forward, keeping your back long.
  • Move slowly enough that you're controlling the motion rather than using momentum.
  • Exhale during the harder part of an exercise; don't hold your breath.
  • Never feel obligated to keep pace with the instructor.

Source: ACSM Resistance Training for Health

How hard should it feel?

For the first two weeks, aim for approximately:

Day typeTarget effort
Dance / cardio4 to 5 out of 10
Strength or Pilates5 to 6 out of 10
Friday recovery2 to 3 out of 10

You should generally be able to speak in sentences. Start slowly and increase gradually, as recommended by the CDC. Change only one thing at a time: duration, speed, range, or weight.

Source: CDC physical activity guidance

If something doesn't feel right

Usually okay Muscles working, warmth, heavier breathing, a gentle stretch, or mild even soreness the following day.
Modify or pause Pinching, joint discomfort, wobbliness, unusual fatigue, or losing your form. Reduce the range, slow down, remove the weights, or hold the chair. If it returns, skip that movement.
Stop the workout Sharp, stabbing, or shooting pain; a sudden pop; swelling; a joint giving way; new numbness or tingling; dizziness; faintness; unusual breathlessness; or heart palpitations.
Call 911 For chest pressure or pain, especially with shortness of breath, nausea, a cold sweat, dizziness, or lightheadedness.

Source: American Heart Association warning signs

If discomfort changes how you walk or sleep, keeps worsening, or repeatedly returns, stop the provoking exercise and consult a healthcare professional or physical therapist. If Tuesday strength leaves your legs significantly sore, replace Wednesday Pilates with gentle standing mobility rather than pushing through it.
