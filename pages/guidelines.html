Guidelines

How to get started, ways to track your progress if that's useful to you, and the participation terms every member of the group agrees to by joining in. For form checkpoints, modifications, and warning signs, see the safety guide.

Getting started

None of the following is required. Some people like having a record to look back on; others would rather just show up and move. Both are complete ways to do this.

Before and after photos

If you'd like a visual record, take a photo on day one and again every few weeks. Wear similar clothing, use similar lighting, and stand in roughly the same spot each time so the photos are easy to compare. Keep them somewhere private; there's no expectation to share them with the group.

Body measurements

Optional, and only if you find it motivating, since the scale and the tape measure don't show strength or mobility gains. The Resources page has a printable body measurement template (chest, waist, hips, thigh, arm, and a notes column) you can use every two to four weeks.

Tracking dietary intake

Also entirely optional. If you want to track what you're eating alongside the workouts, some people track macros (protein, carbs, and fat) and others go further into micros (vitamins and minerals). A few commonly used apps and tools:

  • MyFitnessPal, a widely used food and macro logging app with a large food database.
  • Weight Watchers (WW), a points-based program and app for tracking food and habits.
  • Cronometer, geared toward detailed micronutrient tracking, not just calories and macros.
  • Lose It!, another popular food and calorie logging app.
  • USDA FoodData Central (fdc.nal.usda.gov), a free government database for looking up the nutrient content of specific foods.
  • A plain paper or notes-app food journal, which works just as well as an app for many people.

Pick whichever method, if any, fits how you like to keep track. This is informational, not a recommendation to follow a particular diet or eating pattern; if you have questions about nutrition for your specific situation, a registered dietitian is the right person to ask.

It is always recommended that you consult with, and check with, a physician or other qualified medical or fitness professional before beginning this or any workout routine, especially if you are pregnant, are managing a chronic condition, have had a recent injury or surgery, or have any concerns about your ability to exercise safely.
